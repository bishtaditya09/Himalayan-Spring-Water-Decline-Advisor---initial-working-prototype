# 💧 Himalayan Spring Water Decline Advisor

**AI for Sustainability project — SDG 6 (Clean Water & Sanitation), SDG 13 (Climate
Action), SDG 15 (Life on Land)**

## The problem

Natural springs (*naulas* and *dharas*) are the primary drinking water source for most
hill villages in Uttarakhand and across the Indian Himalayan Region. NITI Aayog's 2018
report on Himalayan springs documented widespread discharge decline — commonly cited in
the 25–50% range — driven by deforestation, unplanned construction disrupting recharge
zones, and shifting rainfall patterns. This is a real, ongoing crisis, not a
hypothetical campus problem.

## What this project does

Two AI components working together:

1. **Forecasting** (`src/forecasting.py`) — a Holt-Winters (triple exponential
   smoothing) time-series model fit per spring, capturing both the long-term decline
   trend and the monsoon-driven seasonal cycle. Produces a 24-month discharge forecast
   and a simple CRITICAL / WATCH / STABLE risk classification relative to each spring's
   own historical baseline.
2. **Retrieval-augmented advisory assistant** (`src/rag_pipeline.py`) — a grounded Q&A
   layer over a curated corpus of real springshed management practices (recharge pits,
   contour trenching, catchment protection, spring mapping, community governance).
   Answers are retrieved and source-attributed, not generated by an LLM — a deliberate
   choice given the real-world consequences of bad water/land intervention advice.

Both are wrapped in an interactive Streamlit app (`app.py`) with three views: a
district-wide risk overview, per-spring forecast detail with charts, and the advisory
Q&A assistant.

## ⚠️ Important data disclosure

Village-level spring discharge records are not publicly available at the granularity
this problem needs. **The discharge and rainfall data used here (`data/springs_synthetic.csv`)
is synthetically generated** (`src/data_generation.py`), grounded in documented trend
ranges (seasonal monsoon recharge patterns, gradual multi-year decline, district-level
variation by forest cover) rather than invented arbitrarily. This is disclosed in the
app itself and should be disclosed in any submission or presentation of this project.
Treat this as a demonstration of the *method* — forecasting + grounded retrieval — not
as real measurements for any specific village or spring.

## Honest limitations (say these out loud in your submission — reviewers respect this)

- **Data is synthetic**, not measured. Real deployment needs an actual spring
  inventory (see `corpus/04_springshed_mapping.txt` for how such a program is
  normally bootstrapped — starting with community-reported observations is a
  realistic, low-cost first step).
- **Retrieval uses TF-IDF, not neural embeddings.** This environment's network
  access couldn't reach a model hub (huggingface.co) at runtime, so a
  Sentence-BERT + vector index approach (identical to the approach used in the
  author's major B.Tech project) wasn't runnable here. `rag_pipeline.py` documents
  exactly where to swap this in — `embed_with_sentence_transformers_STUB()` — for
  a deployment with model access.
- **Risk thresholds are simplified.** The CRITICAL/WATCH/STABLE cutoffs are a
  reasonable simplification for a demo, not a substitute for actual hydrogeological
  risk assessment.
- **The advisory assistant deliberately does not generate free text.** It retrieves
  and surfaces real corpus passages with source attribution instead of using an LLM
  to compose answers, trading conversational fluency for the ability to say every
  claim is traceable to a specific, real intervention practice.

## Responsible AI considerations

- **Fairness:** no demographic or personal data is used or required.
- **Transparency:** every advisory answer names its source document; every
  synthetic-data assumption is documented in `data_generation.py` and surfaced in
  the app UI.
- **Ethics:** the app repeatedly signals that its output is decision-support, not a
  substitute for field hydrogeological survey or expert consultation.
- **Privacy:** no personal or village-identifying data is collected or stored.
