Title: Contour Trenches for Slowing Runoff and Improving Recharge

Contour trenches are shallow, continuous or staggered ditches dug along the natural
contour lines of a hillslope, at right angles to the direction water flows downhill.
Their purpose is to intercept surface runoff during rainfall, hold it in place long
enough to infiltrate, and reduce the soil erosion that strips hillsides of the very
topsoil that helps retain moisture.

Two common patterns are used depending on slope and land use:
- Continuous contour trenches: unbroken ditches along the contour, generally used on
  gentler, more uniform slopes such as grazing land or degraded pasture.
- Staggered (broken) contour trenches: short trench segments arranged in a
  brick-like offset pattern along the contour, preferred on steeper or more
  irregular terrain because a breach in one segment does not cause a cascading
  failure down the whole line.

Typical specifications: trenches are commonly around 30 to 45 centimetres wide and
30 centimetres deep, though this is adapted to local soil depth and rockiness. The
excavated soil is usually mounded on the downhill lip of the trench, which further
slows runoff and can be planted with grass or shrubs to stabilize it.

Placement relative to springs: like recharge pits, contour trenches are most
effective for spring revival when placed within the spring's actual recharge zone,
not simply anywhere convenient. Treating an entire micro-watershed above a spring
with a combination of trenches, pits, and vegetation cover (a "ridge to valley"
treatment approach) tends to outperform isolated structures.

Maintenance: trenches silt up over time, especially in their first two to three
monsoons, and need periodic clearing to keep functioning. Community ownership of
this maintenance is often the deciding factor between a program that keeps working
after the initial construction phase and one that quietly fails within a few years.
